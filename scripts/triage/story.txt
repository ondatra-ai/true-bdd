
`reason` is one sentence, for whoever decides whether to act. `story` is what
they read once they have decided, and it is the ticket's whole value to
someone who was not in the room.

If you score it 6 or above, ALSO return `story`: three short paragraphs, for
a reader who will open these files cold.

  1. How it behaves today. Name the functions and the `file:line` that carry
     the behaviour, and say what they do, in order. Describe the code, not
     the defect.
  2. What goes wrong. The situation that reaches it — the invocation, the
     input, the state left on disk — and the wrong thing that happens then,
     said as an outcome somebody would notice.
  3. Where it is fixed: the file that would be rewritten, and what in it.

Write what you read. Quote the symbol that settles each claim. If you cannot
find the code that produces the behaviour, say that in `story` rather than
inventing a mechanism.

If you score it 5 or below, return `story` empty. A subject about to be
dropped is not worth narrating.

Return `description` empty either way: there is no ticket body to rewrite.
